      About this map and sources

      Detail loads as you zoom: the photo map sharpens to about 1.3 km per pixel, and the four close-up areas to 20–50 m per pixel (100 m in infrared). Heights come from laser altimeter data (about 7 km per sample) and 200 m terrain in close-up areas. A practice build for NASA Space Apps, not an official NASA product.

      • NASA Mars Trek: Viking MDIM 2.1 colour mosaic, MOLA shaded relief
      • NASA PDS: MGS MOLA elevation
      • NASA Mars Trek: MRO CTX mosaics of Jezero, Gale, Olympus Mons and Deuteronilus Mensae
      • USGS Astrogeology: HRSC and MOLA blended 200 m terrain (HRSC: ESA/DLR/FU Berlin, ESA Mars Express; MOLA: NASA/GSFC)
      • IAU/USGS Gazetteer: 1,926 feature names
      • SWIM project: ice consistency maps (60°N to 60°S)
      • Mars Odyssey Neutron Spectrometer, averaged neutron data (NASA PDS Geosciences Node): hydrogen beyond 60°, Mars Years 26–33
      • NASA Mars Trek: Mars Odyssey THEMIS daytime infrared mosaic (100 m)
      • NASA Mars Trek: MGS TES thermal inertia (Putzig et al. 2005) and dust cover index (Ruff & Christensen 2002)
      • NASA Curiosity REMS weather: latest weather reports from Gale crater
      • Hassler et al. 2014 radiation results: Curiosity RAD, 0.67 mSv per day on the surface
      • Belva crater (JPL): Perseverance's 2023 view of Belva's layered walls
      • Gediz Vallis sulfur (NASA): Curiosity's 2024 pure sulfur discovery
      • NASA Mars fact sheet: surface pressure and scale height

          Planning assumptions (change them)

          Resource estimates (simulated, for learning): effort uses the Pandolf load-carrying equation (Pandolf, Givoni & Goldman, 1977) with its weight terms scaled to Mars gravity, and a terrain factor from how firm the ground looks in the thermal-inertia layer. Oxygen assumes 20.1 kJ of energy per litre of O₂. Cooling water assumes 80% of body heat is removed by sublimating water (2.83 MJ/kg). Suit O₂ and water capacity are sized for your suit-time setting at a moderate 350 W work rate. The 8.7 kWh rover battery default matches the Apollo Lunar Roving Vehicle's two 121 Ah, 36 V batteries; rover mass 900 kg, 70% drivetrain efficiency and 200 W electronics are guesses. None of these are real spacesuit or rover specifications.

          Why this walking model: Tobler's hiking function is a standard, well-tested formula for how slope changes human walking speed (fastest on a gentle downhill, slower up or down steep ground). Suits are stiff and heavy, so the default ×0.5 pace (about 2.5 km/h on the flat) is a deliberately cautious guess. Change it to test faster or slower crews. Rover speed is a flat cruise speed, and slopes steeper than your limit are walked. These are planning guesses, not suit or rover specifications.

          Path choices search the terrain between waypoints (20 m terrain at Jezero, 200 m in the other close-ups). Safest terrain: quickest route that stays under your slope limit. Fastest: quickest route, ignoring the slope limit. Resource-saving: uses the least suit oxygen and battery (and rover battery), avoiding steep and soft ground, while staying under the slope limit. Straight line: no search.

          A concept planner. Terrain is 200 m resolution in the close-up areas and about 7 km elsewhere, so boulders, small scarps and sand ripples don't show. Scout any real route with high-resolution imagery first.
